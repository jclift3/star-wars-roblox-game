# Campaign

The story the game is telling, who you are in it, and who you meet. Written
2026-08-14 after four decisions (era, origins, cameos, map authoring) were
settled.

Companion documents: [PLANETS.md](PLANETS.md) for what each world contains,
[ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md) for build order.

---

## 1. The setting

**The Old Republic era. 3,640 years before the Battle of Yavin.**

"~3600 BBY" pins to the most usable year in that window: the Cold War has just
collapsed back into open war. The Treaty of Coruscant was signed thirteen years
ago, after the Sith Empire sacked the capital and dictated terms from the ruins
of the Jedi Temple. Nobody believes the peace. Everybody is preparing.

Why this era earns its keep:

- **A Sith orphan is normal here, not an exception.** The Academy on Korriban is
  a school that takes children and kills most of them. In the film era there are
  two Sith in the galaxy; here there are thousands, with a bureaucracy, a
  curriculum, and a waiting list. The origin story asked for is native to the
  setting.
- **Both sides are governments.** You can be a loyal Imperial who is also the
  hero, which the film era does not allow. A Sith player and a Republic player
  can co-op without one of them being a traitor.
- **The Force trees mean something.** Jedi and Sith are institutions with ranks,
  not a secret. `Progression`'s Force tree stops being a strange bonus and
  becomes a career.
- **Nar Shaddaa is at its peak,** the Hutt Cartel is a great power rather than
  one slug in a palace, and `lawLevel = 0.0` is a design statement instead of
  flavour.
- **Coruscant is half-ruined,** which the authored `Underlevels` zone already
  implies and the film era does not support.

### Era rules

| Rule | Consequence |
|---|---|
| No lightsaber is common | A saber is a rank, not a purchase. Revisit `SaberBlue` costing 7,500 credits. |
| Hyperspace is slow and mapped | Travel between planets is a journey with a cost, which is what Phase 2 wants anyway. |
| Droids are old and strange | HK-series assassin droids, war-surplus, memory-wiped. Better characters than B1s. |
| Czerka Corporation sells to both sides | The era's third power and our corporate villain. |
| Mandalorians are a culture, not a bounty hunter | The Great Hunt is an institution you can enter. |

## 2. The spine: The Quiet

> Something is unmaking Force-sensitives. One at a time, quietly, across both
> empires. It looks like a disease. It is a harvest.

An acolyte drops mid-duel and gets up hollow — alive, talking, and unable to feel
anything ever again. A Jedi youngling stops being able to lift a cup. A Hutt's
pet seer goes blank in the middle of a sentence and the Hutt has her thrown out
with the rubbish, because she is worthless now.

Both governments know. Both are covering it up, because the first one to admit
it is losing has lost. The Sith Empire is quietly executing the hollowed as
defectives. The Republic is quietly filing them as combat fatigue. Czerka
Corporation is quietly *buying them*, which is the thread you pull.

The Force is not leaving these people. It is being **collected**.

### Why this spine and not another

Four properties, each doing a job the game already needs:

1. **It threatens Sith and Jedi equally,** so four origins on opposite sides of a
   war can converge without anyone changing their politics. This is the
   convergence device.
2. **It cannot touch the non-Force origins.** The soldier, the smuggler and the
   mechanic are the only people in the galaxy who are safe — which makes them
   the only people who can go where a Jedi cannot. The roadmap wants
   "complementary co-op classes"; this is that requirement expressed as story
   rather than as a stat sheet. A Force player and a non-Force player are not
   two flavours of the same character, they are a lockpick and a lock.
3. **It gives a moral axis that is not politeness.** The endgame question is not
   "were you nice." It is: do you want to *stop* the harvest, or *inherit* it?
   A player can be a decent Sith who stops it and a monstrous Jedi who does not.
4. **It is a mystery, so it can be authored in pieces.** Each planet holds one
   fact. The game is playable and coherent after two of them.

### The answer, for our eyes only

The collector is not a person. It is a **place**: a world scraped clean of the
Force millennia ago by a Sith ritual that never finished, and has been slowly
pulling ever since — a wound that drinks. It has no will and cannot be
negotiated with. What it *does* have is a caretaker: a Sith Lord who found it a
century ago, learned to aim it, and has been feeding on the surplus. He is old,
tired, and entirely sane, which is worse.

The final choice: seal the wound (and every hollowed person stays hollow
forever), or finish the ritual and take the caretaker's place (and get all of it,
and become the next thing that has to be stopped). There is a third ending for a
player who spent the whole game building relationships instead of power — the
hollowed can be *given back*, one at a time, by someone willing to give up their
own connection to the Force. It should cost the player everything the Force tree
bought them, and it should be the best ending.

## 3. Origins

Four, one per existing skill tree, so this adds no new axis to the character
sheet. Each is a ~20 minute prologue on its own planet, its own mentor, its own
starting gear, and a personal thread that recurs for the rest of the campaign.

They converge on Tatooine in Act 1. **Four prologues, not four games.**

### Acolyte — Force tree

**Starts:** Korriban, the Academy.
**Was:** taken as an infant from a world that no longer appears on charts. Raised
by the Academy, which is to say raised by nobody. Has never had a name that
someone else did not assign.
**Mentor:** *Overseer Vashk*, who is not kind and is not lying to you. He tells
you on day one that most of you will die here and that this is the point.
**Prologue:** three trials in the Valley of the Dark Lords. On the last, your
only friend in the Academy goes hollow in front of you and Vashk has her put
down as defective without breaking stride. You are sent off-world to keep quiet
about it.
**Thread:** you are the only person alive who saw it happen and cared.

### Conscript — Combat tree

**Starts:** Ord Mantell (or Coruscant's surface if we cut a planet).
**Was:** signed up at fifteen out of a resettlement block, because the Republic
Army fed you. Competent, unglamorous, and outranked by everyone.
**Mentor:** *Sergeant Tolen Marr*, who has been in for twenty years and is the
first adult who has ever told you the truth.
**Prologue:** a garrison action that goes wrong. Your unit's Jedi liaison goes
hollow mid-firefight and you are the one who has to drag her out. Command files
it as fatigue and orders you not to write it down.
**Thread:** you have an official document that says the thing did not happen, and
your own memory that it did.

### Scoundrel — Piloting tree

**Starts:** Nar Shaddaa.
**Was:** born owing money. Your parents' debt, transferred, with interest, which
is legal on Nar Shaddaa.
**Mentor:** *Vess Kadar*, Twi'lek fixer, who owns the debt and is genuinely fond
of you, which does not change the debt.
**Prologue:** a courier run. The cargo is a sedated Force-sensitive girl, and you
were not supposed to look. Whatever you do about it, Czerka does not get its
delivery and you are now on a list.
**Thread:** you know what the cargo manifests look like and can read them.

### Scrapper — Engineering tree

**Starts:** Taris, the ruined city.
**Was:** raised in a salvage crew picking over a dead planet. Talks to machines
more comfortably than to people.
**Mentor:** *Ordo-9* ("Nine"), an HK-series assassin droid you dug out of a
collapsed tower and repaired without knowing what it was. It remembers almost
nothing and is very polite about the things it does remember.
**Prologue:** a Czerka survey team is stripping your dig site. In the wreck
underneath, you find a sealed container built to hold a person and monitor them.
**Thread:** you have the container, and Nine has fragments of a memory of
delivering others like it.

## 4. Acts

Level bands line up with `PlanetDef.minLevel/maxLevel` and with each planet's
`ZoneDef.distance`, so walking outward is walking into harder things.

| Act | Levels | Planets | The question it answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0 — Origins** | 1–6 | one per origin | Who are you, and what did you see? |
| **1 — The First Body** | 6–15 | Tatooine | Is it happening to other people too? |
| **2 — The Buyers** | 15–26 | Nar Shaddaa, Taris | Who is paying for it? |
| **3 — The Cover** | 26–36 | Coruscant, Tython | Why does nobody in charge want it found? |
| **4 — The Method** | 36–46 | Korriban, Hoth | How is it being done, and by whom? |
| **5 — The Wound** | 46–50 | Dromund Kaas, then the dead world | What are you going to do about it? |

### Act 1 — The First Body (Tatooine, 6–15)

Everyone lands here, whatever their origin, which is why Tatooine is the planet
to finish first: it is already the most built, and it has to work for all four
prologues.

A Jawa clan scavenged a Czerka transport that came down in the Dune Sea, and sold
the salvage all over Mos Eisley before anyone realised one of the crates had a
person in it. She is hollow, she is alive, and she is now in Jabba's collection
because a Hutt will buy anything unusual.

Beats: find who bought the crates → the moisture farmers who found her →
the cantina, where a Mandalorian on the Great Hunt is looking for the same
transport for different reasons → Jabba's Palace, where you can buy her,
steal her, or trade her for something worse.

Ends with a Czerka recovery team arriving to take her back, and a shipping code
on their manifest that means Nar Shaddaa.

### Act 2 — The Buyers (Nar Shaddaa and Taris, 15–26)

Two planets, either order, which is where the world stops being a corridor.

**Nar Shaddaa** is the market: Force-sensitives have a price, the Cartel takes a
cut, and Czerka is not the only buyer. This is the Scoundrel's home ground and
the act where Vess Kadar stops being a creditor and becomes a choice.

**Taris** is the warehouse: a ruined planet nobody audits, full of sealed
containers in a building Czerka does not admit owning. This is the Scrapper's
home ground and where Nine starts remembering.

Ends with Doctor Aneth Corr, the Czerka officer running procurement, who is not
a sadist. She has a budget, a quota, and a signed contract with a client she has
never met, and she will explain all of it because none of it is illegal.

### Act 3 — The Cover (Coruscant and Tython, 26–36)

You now have a client's account number and nowhere to take it. Both governments
turn out to already know.

**Coruscant** — you take it to the Republic. The Senate district is intact and
polished; the Underlevels are still rubble thirteen years after the Sacking.
Someone senior thanks you sincerely and buries it, because a Republic that
admits its Jedi are being farmed is a Republic that gets invaded again.

**Tython** — you take it to the Jedi. *Master Ryn Solaa* is the first person who
names the thing out loud and the first who is glad you came. The Council is not
glad. This is where the game states its actual theme: institutions protect
themselves first, and the people who help you will be individuals acting against
their own side.

Ends in betrayal by whoever you trusted, and a fixed point either way: you are
now doing this without a government.

### Act 4 — The Method (Korriban and Hoth, 36–46)

**Korriban** is the library. The ritual is old and written down, in a tomb in the
Valley of the Dark Lords that the Academy teaches students not to enter. The
Acolyte comes home and meets Overseer Vashk again, who has known for years.

**Hoth** is the evidence. The starship graveyard — thousands of hulls from a
battle that was fought over nothing anyone can now explain — hides the transport
that has been carrying the collected. It has been making the same run for a
century.

Ends with a destination that is not on any chart.

### Act 5 — The Wound (46–50)

**Dromund Kaas** for the caretaker's household and the last living witnesses.
Then the dead world, which has no zones, no NPCs, no weather and no sound, and
where the Force tree's abilities simply do not function — the one place in the
game where a Sith player is the weakest person in the room and has to be carried.

Three endings, above.

## 5. The recurring cast

The crossover the design asks for. Six characters, each introduced as one
origin's intimate and encountered by the other three as a stranger. Playing a
second origin should re-contextualise people you thought you knew.

| Character | Origin they belong to | What the other three see |
|---|---|---|
| **Overseer Vashk** — Sith, Korriban | Acolyte's mentor | The Academy official who signs the disposal orders. An antagonist until Act 4 reveals he has been documenting it for thirty years and can do nothing. |
| **Sgt. Tolen Marr** — Republic, human | Conscript's sergeant | A stubborn investigator working a case nobody assigned him. Turns up wherever the Republic officially isn't. |
| **Vess Kadar** — Twi'lek fixer, Nar Shaddaa | Scoundrel's creditor | The only person who will sell you what you need, at a price you resent. Act 2 makes her pick a side. |
| **Ordo-9 "Nine"** — HK-series droid | Scrapper's companion | A very courteous assassin droid you have to survive before you can talk to it. Best companion candidate; the boys will love it. |
| **Master Ryn Solaa** — Jedi, Tython | nobody's — she is the shared ally | Introduced identically to all four in Act 3. The one who names the Quiet. |
| **Dr. Aneth Corr** — Czerka | nobody's — she is the shared antagonist | Procurement. Reasonable, cooperative, and the reason it works at scale. Not a boss fight; a person you can talk into or out of things. |

### Cameos

Policy is **cameos only — you stay a nobody.** Famous faces are weather and set
pieces: they make the galaxy real without your story competing with a better
known one. Roughly one per act, never a party member, never a fight you win.

- **Darth Malgus** — the face of the Sacking. Act 3, Coruscant: he walks through
  a room you are hiding in. You survive by not being noticed. No health bar.
- **Grand Master Satele Shan** — Act 3, Tython. One conversation, in which she
  declines to help and is right to.
- **HK-47** — findable on Taris, in pieces, in a Czerka lot. Nine's ancestor and
  the game's best gag. Optional, missable, worth the trouble.
- **Mandalore the Vindicated** — Act 1 and again in Act 4. Sanctions your entry
  into the Great Hunt, which is how a Combat player gets their endgame weapon.
- **Nico Okarr** — Nar Shaddaa. Sells you a bad ship and a good tip.
- **Revan** — never appears. Referenced constantly, wrongly, by people who did
  not know him. Three characters give you three incompatible accounts.
- **The tombs** — Marka Ragnos, Naga Sadow, Tulak Hord, Exar Kun. History you
  walk through rather than meet.

## 6. Systems the story needs

Ordered by how much they cost. Each is small; the story is what is expensive.

### 6.1 Origin — new

`PlayerProfile.origin: string`, chosen at character creation, one of
`Acolyte | Conscript | Scoundrel | Scrapper`. Needs:

- `Config/Origins.luau` — display name, blurb, start planet, start weapon,
  start outfit, mentor archetype, and the skill node it starts with at rank 1.
- `ObjectiveDef` and dialogue `Condition` gain `origin: string?`, so one mission
  and one conversation can serve all four and say something different to each.
  This is the cheap version of four stories.
- A creation screen, following the existing 760x470 panel convention.

### 6.2 Alignment — new

`PlayerProfile.alignment: number`, clamped -1000..1000. Moved by dialogue
choices and by mission resolutions, not by combat. Gates the Force tree's deep
nodes, the saber crystal colours already in `Weapons.luau`, and the endings.

Distinct from `factionRep`, and the distinction is the point: a Sith at +800
alignment is a legitimate and interesting character.

### 6.3 Acts and chapters — mostly free

`MissionDef` already has `requires`, `next`, `minLevel` and `requiredRep`, and
`Missions.boardFor` already respects all four. A campaign is a correctly wired
`requires` graph. What is missing:

- `MissionDef.act: number` and `.chapter: number?`, for ordering the board and
  for a "Chapter 2: The Buyers" card on screen.
- `MissionDef.origin: string?` for the four prologues.
- A journal view. The mission board is a to-do list; a campaign needs a record of
  what happened, because the story is a mystery and the player will forget.

### 6.4 Consequence — the expensive one

Faction rep is awarded by missions and never read back (a known gap). The story
above requires at minimum:

- NPC attitude and dialogue varying by rep (`NPCBrain.isEnemy` already reads
  attitude; dialogue conditions already support `minRep`).
- Mission availability varying by *choices*, not only by level — which needs a
  `flags: { [string]: boolean }` on the profile and a `flag` condition. One
  field, and it unlocks all branching.

### 6.5 Companions — Phase 5

Nine is the argument for them. Not required for the campaign to work.

## 7. Migration cost

Honest accounting of what the era decision breaks. Nothing here is hard; it is
find-and-replace plus mission rewriting that was needed regardless.

**Planets — 4 replaced.** Naboo → **Tython**, Kamino → **Taris**, Mustafar →
**Korriban**, Endor → **Dromund Kaas**. Tatooine, Coruscant, Hoth and Nar Shaddaa
keep their `PlanetDef` almost unchanged. See [PLANETS.md](PLANETS.md).

**Archetypes — 8 renamed, 1 cut, ~6 added.**

| Now | Becomes |
|---|---|
| Stormtrooper | Imperial Trooper |
| Sandtrooper | Imperial Scout |
| DeathTrooper | Sith Trooper |
| RoyalGuard | Imperial Guard |
| ImperialOfficer | *unchanged* |
| RebelTrooper / RebelPilot | Republic Trooper / Republic Pilot |
| CloneVeteran | Republic Veteran |
| BountyHunter | Mandalorian Hunter |
| Scientist (Kaminoan) | Czerka Scientist |
| NabooGuard | Republic Marshal |
| JediSurvivor | Jedi Knight (an institution now, not a fugitive) |
| BattleDroid | **cut** — replaced by Czerka Security Droid and HK-series |

New: Sith Overseer, Sith Inquisitor, Czerka Foreman, Cartel Slicer, Hollowed
(an unarmed, harmless, deeply unsettling wandering NPC), Assassin Droid.

**Factions — 1 renamed, 1 repurposed, 1 added.** `Rebellion` → `Republic`
(military), the existing `Republic` → **`Jedi`** (the Order), `Empire` → the
**Sith Empire** (id can stay `Empire`), `Hutt` unchanged, `Independent`
unchanged, and **`Czerka`** added as the sixth. Rank names in `Factions.luau`
need rewriting; the spillover matrix is structurally right as-is.

**Missions — all 15 rewritten.** They are currently 5 unconnected chains with no
theme. The three Tatooine missions are the closest to salvageable and map onto
Act 1 with new text.

**Weapons, outfits, items, skills, dialogue plumbing, and every service —
untouched.** The engine does not care what era it is.

## 8. Open questions

- **Prologue planets.** Four origins want four start planets, and Conscript's
  (Ord Mantell) would be a ninth world built for 20 minutes of play. Cheaper:
  start the Conscript on Coruscant's surface and the Scrapper on Taris, both of
  which are needed anyway. Decide before building Act 0.
- **Does the player get a lightsaber, and when?** Era rules say a saber is a
  rank. Current `Weapons.luau` sells one at level 10 for 7,500 credits. If the
  saber is earned rather than bought, that is a genuine reward moment and a
  reason for the Force tree to exist; if it is bought, the Acolyte origin has no
  climax.
- **How dark is too dark?** This is a game for your sons. The Quiet involves
  children being bought, and the Academy kills students. That is *Star Wars*-dark
  rather than grim, and the same register as Order 66, but it is your call where
  the line is and it should be set before the dialogue is written.
- **The game's name.** `OuterRimOdyssey` was always a placeholder and does not
  fit this era at all — none of the eight planets are Outer Rim except Tatooine
  and Korriban. Wants a name from this setting.
